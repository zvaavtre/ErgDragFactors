# TL;DR: 2k Stroke Length, Rate & Drag Factor Playbook (By Height & Style)

No equations—just actionable rules for setting your Concept2 PM5 **Drag Factor** (`More Options > Display Drag Factor`) and managing your **Stroke Length** and **Rate** when your legs start burning in a 2,000m test.

---

## 1. Quick Setup by Rower Height & Lever Length

Your height and wingspan dictate how long the chain travels (`Drive Length` in ErgData) and how much mass you move up and down the slide every stroke.

### Tall Rowers ($6'2"\text{+} / 188\text{ cm+}$) — *Target Drive Length: $1.45 - 1.60\text{ m}$*
* **Target Drag Factor:** **125 – 138** *(Damper ~4.5 – 5.5)*
* **Base 2k Rate:** **30 – 34 spm**
* **Your Biggest Advantage:** Massive work per stroke ($W$). You don't need to rate 38 spm to pull a fast split.
* **Your Biggest Trap:** Setting the drag too low (<115) or rushing the slide. Moving a long, heavy body up and down the rail 36+ times a minute wastes huge aerobic energy just accelerating and decelerating your own body mass.
* **What to Do When Tired (1,000m–1,500m):** **Do not rush the slide to jack the rate up.** Keep your recovery deliberate so the fan slows down and catches your heavy legs right at the front. Push the legs down hard and use your long layback lever.

### Medium-Height Rowers ($5'9" - 6'1" / 175 - 185\text{ cm}$) — *Target Drive Length: $1.30 - 1.45\text{ m}$*
* **Target Drag Factor:** **115 – 128** *(Damper ~3.5 – 4.5)*
* **Base 2k Rate:** **33 – 36 spm**
* **Your Biggest Advantage:** Versatile gearing. You can shift between power strokes and high turnover without the massive slide-energy penalty of a $6'6"$ rower.
* **Your Biggest Trap:** Over-reaching at the catch (letting your shins go past $90^\circ$ vertical and lifting your heels excessively) trying to match a tall rower's `Drive Length`. Over-compression kills your knee leverage and causes **catch slip** (the fan spins away before your legs engage).
* **What to Do When Tired (1,000m–1,500m):** Focus on **quick hands away** from the body at the finish so you don't have to rush the last 6 inches into the catch. Stop your shins at vertical ($90^\circ$) and snap the heels down immediately.

### Shorter / Compact Rowers ($\text{Under } 5'9" / <175\text{ cm}$) — *Target Drive Length: $1.10 - 1.28\text{ m}$*
* **Target Drag Factor:** **105 – 118** *(Damper ~3.0 – 4.0)*
* **Base 2k Rate:** **35 – 40 spm**
* **Your Biggest Advantage:** Low slide inertia and rapid recovery turnaround. You can hold high stroke rates at a much lower metabolic cost than tall rowers.
* **Your Biggest Trap:** **Cranking the damper up (6–10) to "make up" for a shorter stroke.** Because your stroke is shorter, generating a fast split at a high drag factor requires unsustainable peak force on every single stroke, blowing up your quads and lower back by the 800m mark.
* **What to Do When Tired (1,000m–1,500m):** Trust your turnover. Keep the drag light (105–115), stay upright at the catch (don't lung-collapse over your knees), and keep the handle moving continuously without pausing at the finish or catch.

## 2. Adjustments by Rowing Style (Force vs. Turnover)

Two rowers of the exact same height often produce watts differently. Pick the column that matches your physiology:

| Feature | **"Diesel / Aerobic / Tempo" Rower** *(Cardio-dominant, lighter frame, cyclist/runner background)* | **"Power / Strength" Rower** *(Muscle-dominant, heavy squat/deadlift, explosive)* |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Drag Factor Adjustment** | Subtract **5–8 points** from your height bracket (e.g., 112–120). | Add **5–8 points** to your height bracket (e.g., 128–138). |
| **How You Make Speed** | Keeping the flywheel spinning fast ($\omega_{\text{min}}$ stays high) so no single stroke feels heavy. | Letting the flywheel slow down slightly more on the recovery so you have a "heavy wall" to push against. |
| **Main Technical Cue** | **"Quick catch"** — your hands and legs must reverse direction instantly at the front so you don't pull air (slip) on a fast-spinning fan. | **"Patience on the slide"** — drive fast, then float up the slide so your fast-twitch fibers get $0.1\text{s}$ of blood flow before the next heavy press. |
| **How to Survive the 3rd 500m** | Hold your rate steady ($35+$ spm) and **cut 2 cm off your catch compression** if your legs feel sluggish at the turnaround. | **Drop or hold rate ($31–33$ spm)** for 15 strokes and focus on full leg extension and layback swing—do not chop the stroke short. |

## 3. The 4 Golden Rules of 2k Rate & Slip Management

1. **Never Rushing the Slide When Exhausted (1,000m–1,600m):**
   When you're tired, your legs react slower at the catch. If you rush up the slide to raise the rate, the flywheel hasn't slowed down yet—so your tired legs meet a *faster* flywheel. Result: **massive catch slip** (you pull 15–20 cm of dead air before the chain bites). Keep the slide controlled so the fan slows down to meet your legs.
2. **How to Sprint the Last 350m Without Slipping:**
   In the final sprint, your legs can no longer push a heavy load, so you *must* raise the rate ($+3\text{ to }6\text{ spm}$). To stop the fast flywheel from slipping away from your tired legs at 38+ spm, **deliberately stop 2–3 cm short of full compression at the catch**. Slightly wider knee angles give you instant leverage to grab the fast fan.
3. **Never Over-Reach for Extra `Drive Length`:**
   Lunging your shoulders past your hips or letting your shins go past $90^\circ$ vertical adds "fake" reach where your body has zero leverage. Those first 5 cm of weak movement just let the flywheel spin down without adding watts.
4. **Watch ErgData's `Drive Length` in Practice:**
   If your `Drive Length` drops by more than **$5\text{ cm}$** between the first 500m and third 500m of a piece, your drag factor is either too high (stalling your muscles) or you are rushing the slide and losing the catch.
