# References

Every recommendation in this repository comes from a source below. Each source was opened on 2026-10-30, and each entry says if only part was read.

## How to set your drag factor

### Concept2: What Damper Setting Should I Use?
* **Link:** <https://concept2.com/news/what-damper-setting-should-i-use> (published 2019-04-15)
* **Says:** The best setting is personal preference and whatever gets you the best results. Recommends damper 3–5, with 3 for longer and 5 for shorter workouts. Damper 10 is "rarely (if ever) recommended". Very low settings need a quick, light catch. High settings are difficult to sustain with effective technique and may lead to injury. Gives a 1-minute-on, 1-minute-off test for trying settings.

### Concept2: Viewing Drag Factor
* **Link:** <https://www.concept2.nl/en/service/monitors/pm5/how-to-use/viewing-drag-factor> (Concept2 Netherlands site)
* **Says:** Main Menu → More Options → Display Drag Factor, then row. The monitor shows the number after a few seconds. The damper lever adjusts it.

### Concept2: What Damper Setting and Drag Factor to Use
* **Link:** <https://concept2.com/training/articles/damper-setting>
* **Says:** Start at damper 3–5. A lower setting may give the best workout as technique improves. Setting the lever too high can tire muscles before the cardiovascular system gets the full benefit.

### Concept2: What is the Best Damper Setting for Me?
* **Link:** <https://concept2.com/blog/what-is-the-best-damper-setting-for-me> (published 2025-01-03)
* **Says:** A new RowErg reads 90 or less at damper 1 and 200 or more at damper 10. Damper numbers differ between machines, so compare by drag factor.

### Concept2: The Damper and Drag of Olympians
* **Link:** <https://www.concept2.com/blog/the-damper-and-drag-of-olympians> (published 2018-07-30)
* **Says:** Interviews with Olympic rowers and coaches. Reported drag factors run from about 112 to 130 for the Olympians. One recommends "around 120-135 (depending on how big and strong the athlete is)" and advises testing on the setting you train with.

### Concept2: Drive Length
* **Link:** <https://www.concept2.com/blog/drive-length> (published 2017-08-02)
* **Says:** There is no perfect drive length. The best length varies with height, leg length and flexibility. A longer drive can make a high stroke rate harder to keep. A shorter-than-normal stroke may point to poor warm-up, fatigue or a technique change.

### Concept2 Indoor Rower Fact Sheet 1
* **Link:** <https://www.leicester-rowing.co.uk/media/documents/rofo/Concept2_Setting_The_Drag_Factor.pdf> (*Damper Lever, Drag Factor and the Speed Strap*, Fletcher Sport Science; copy hosted by a third party, undated)
* **Says:** Adults: drag factor 115–140. On-water rowers: 125–140, because the feel is closest to a racing boat. Set the same drag factor every session. Too high a setting increases injury risk.

## Body size and drag factor

None of these sources gives measured data pairing drag factor with height.

### Concept2 forum: Optimal drag factor vs demographics data?
* **Link:** <https://www.c2forum.com/viewtopic.php?t=12829> (2012)
* **Says:** A rower asks for data relating drag factor to height, weight and gender. Nobody provides any, and a Concept2 technician replies that there are too many variables.

### Concept2 forum: Drag Factor
* **Link:** <https://www.c2forum.com/viewtopic.php?t=184580> (2018)
* **Says:** One poster writes, "The older and taller you are, the more you'll want low drag." This is anecdote.

### Coaching article: What Drag Factor Should You Use
* **Link:** <https://www.cattrenthamrowing.com/post/what-drag-factor-should-you-use-and-should-it-change-by-distance> (2025)
* **Says:** Taller, heavier, strength-trained or more experienced rowers usually sit near the top of the range. Shorter, lighter or newer rowers usually sit lower. The text gives no data for this.

### Online drag factor tools
* **Links:** <https://www.rowhq.app/uk/tools/drag-factor-calculator>; <https://rowingregimen.com/tools/drag-factor>; <https://www.rojabo.com/drag-factor/>
* **Says:** Neither calculator asks for height, and neither shows a formula. Their inputs include body weight and experience. Rojabo recommends testing and says there is no single correct answer.

## Published drag factors by category

### British Rowing safety alert (2018)
* **Source:** *Safety Alert – take care setting up your C2 rowing machine*, British Rowing, April 2018.
* **Link:** <https://www.britishrowing.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/Safety-Alert-take-care-setting-up-your-C2-rowing-machine-1.pdf>
* **Says:** Recommended ranges by sex and weight, and by school year for children. This is the only source here that states weight cut-offs.

### Rowing Australia: 2000m and 5000m Ergometer Test Protocols
* **Link:** <https://rowingaustralia.com.au/hubfs/2000m-and-5000m-Ergometer-Protocol-2020-Onwards.pdf>
* **Says:** Fixed test settings by category. Junior and lightweight women 100, heavyweight women 110, junior and lightweight men 125, heavyweight men 130.

### Rowing Canada Aviron: National NextGen Testing and Monitoring Strategy 2021–2024
* **Link:** <https://rowingcanada.org/uploads/2020/12/Testing-and-Monitoring_Nov2020_FINAL.pdf>
* **Says:** Fixed test settings by category (Appendix D). All women's categories 110, junior and lightweight men 120, open men 130.

### Concept2 NZ: Making Sense of Drag Factor and Resistance
* **Link:** <https://www.concept2.co.nz/Blog/x_post/Making-sense-of-drag-factor-and-resistance-on-the-indoor-rower-00092.html> (published 2018-06-05)
* **Says:** National erg testing in New Zealand uses 130 for men and 110 for women. This is a secondary report, as we did not find the Rowing New Zealand protocol. The post also says no single setting suits everyone.

## Research

### Cerasola et al. (2017)
* **Citation:** Cerasola, D., Cataldo, A., Bianco, A., Zangla, D., Capranica, L., & Traina, M. (2017). Drag factor on rowing ergometer during 2000-m performance in young rowers. *Kinesiologia Slovenica*, 23(2), 15–21.
* **Link:** <https://www.dlib.si/details/URN:NBN:SI:DOC-PDEI3LO6> (full text read)
* **Design:** 15 male Italian championship finalists (about 16 years, 177.4 ± 6.4 cm, 72.7 ± 9.6 kg) rowed maximal 2,000m tests at 110, 130 and 150 in randomized order, 24 hours apart.
* **Results (Table 1):**

| Drag factor | Time | Stroke rate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 110 | 435.0 ± 22.7 s | 35.1 ± 0.8 spm |
| 130 | 419.1 ± 24.1 s | 36.7 ± 0.7 spm |
| 150 | 433.7 ± 24.5 s | 32.7 ± 0.7 spm |

* **Body mass (Figure 2):** The published regression lines are time = 570.71 − 1.87 × mass at 110, 575.36 − 2.15 × mass at 130, and 588.92 − 2.13 × mass at 150. They predict 459, 446 and 461 s at 60 kg, and 403, 382 and 397 s at 90 kg, so 130 is fastest at both ends. The predictions are our arithmetic, not the authors'.
* **Says:** Heavier rowers were faster at all three settings. The authors conclude the results support 130 "independently from the anthropometric characteristics of youth athletes". They also repeat a recommendation of 100 for youth and novice rowers and 140 for experienced heavyweights, citing a 2004 Concept2 training guide and Hahn et al. (2000).
* **Limits:** The methods list only Pearson correlations, so no significance test of the differences between settings is reported. The study also did not test whether the best setting changes with body size. The authors call the sample small.
* **Errors in the paper:** The abstract says stroke rate was highest at 110, but its own numbers, Table 1 and the discussion show 130. The abstract gives height correlations that Table 1 lists as stroke rate vs. body mass, so we do not cite them. The 150 time's SD differs between the abstract (25.2) and Table 1 (24.5).

### Metikos et al. (2015)
* **Citation:** Metikos, B., Mikulic, P., Sarabon, N., & Markovic, G. (2015). Peak power output test on a rowing ergometer: a methodological study. *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*, 29(10), 2919–2925.
* **Link:** <https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU201510008062> (abstract). Only the abstract was read.
* **Says:** 87 participants did short maximal efforts at three settings. Rowers produced their highest power at the moderate setting, while non-rowers peaked at the highest. The abstract reports nothing on body size.

### Kane et al. (2008)
* **Citation:** Kane, D. A., Jensen, R. L., Williams, S. E., & Watts, P. B. (2008). Effects of drag factor on physiological aspects of rowing. *International Journal of Sports Medicine*, 29(5), 390–394. DOI [10.1055/s-2007-965333](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-2007-965333)
* **PubMed:** <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17990205/>
* **Says:** 14 club rowers did incremental tests at 100 and 150. Submaximal stages showed no significant differences. Maximal ventilation was higher at 100, and the ventilation difference tracked stroke rate. Only the abstract was read.

### Kane et al. (2013)
* **Citation:** Kane, D. A., Mackenzie, S. J., Jensen, R. L., & Watts, P. B. (2013). Effects of stroke resistance on rowing economy in club rowers post-season. *International Journal of Sports Medicine*, 34(2), 131–137. DOI [10.1055/s-0032-1321721](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0032-1321721)
* **PubMed:** <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22895868/>
* **Says:** 12 club rowers (5 men, 7 women) did incremental tests at 100 and 150. The low setting gave higher cadence and heart rate. Economy was better at the high setting. The authors conclude that greater resistance may benefit performance. Only the abstract was read.

### Kleshnev: How DF on Ergometer correlates with on-water rowing?
* **Link:** <https://biorow.com/comparison-of-on-water-rowing-with-machines-2/> (BioRow, 2026-04-04); reprinted at <https://www.row2k.com/features/6784/how-drag-factor-on-ergometer-correlates-with-onwater-rowing/>
* **Says:** Handle Drag Factor (HDF) depends strongly on rower weight on the water. On the erg it has almost no correlation with rower weight or power, and it tracks damper and drag factor almost perfectly. Erg settings have no one-to-one match with a boat type. Only the public version of this newsletter was read.

### Kleshnev: Handle Drag Factor
* **Link:** <https://old.biorow.com/index.php?news_id=54&route=information%2Fnews%2Fnews> (RBN 2020/01)
* **Says:** HDF, introduced in RBN 2011/01, is the ratio of rowing power to the cube of average handle velocity over the drive. The original 2011 issue was not read.

### Dudhia: The Physics of Rowing
* **Link:** <http://eodg.atm.ox.ac.uk/user/dudhia/rowing/physics/ergometer.html>
* **Says:** Defines drag factor from flywheel deceleration during recovery. The monitor measures actual damping, so it accounts for vent setting and air pressure. Power follows $P = c u^3$, with Concept2 quoting $c = 2.8$. The page does not advise on choosing a drag factor.
