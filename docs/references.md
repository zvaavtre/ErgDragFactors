# References

Every recommendation in this repository comes from a source below. Each entry gives the citation and where to get the source. All links were tested on 2026-10-30, and each entry says if only part was read.

## Research archive

### UCLA Bionics Lab Rowing Archive
* **Citation:** UCLA Bionics Lab. Rowing paper archive.
* **Get it:** [open directory](http://bionics.seas.ucla.edu/education/Rowing/)
* **What it is:** An open directory of 165 rowing papers in PDF form, hosted by the UCLA Bionics Lab. It covers physiology (48), biomechanics (42), training (31), mathematical models (19), technique (14), injury (8) and rigging (3).
* **Why it is useful:** It is a good first stop for full texts that are hard to find elsewhere. It holds copies of [Soper & Hume (2004)](#soper--hume-2004) and [Kane et al. (2013)](#kane-et-al-2013).
* **What we checked:** We searched all 165 files by text, but did not read them all. Only Kane et al. (2013) relates drag factor to rower size.

## How to set your drag factor

### Concept2: What Damper Setting Should I Use?
* **Citation:** Concept2. (2019, April 15). *What Damper Setting Should I Use?*
* **Get it:** [Concept2](https://concept2.com/news/what-damper-setting-should-i-use)
* **Says:** The best setting is personal preference and whatever gets you the best results. Recommends damper 3–5, with 3 for longer and 5 for shorter workouts. Damper 10 is "rarely (if ever) recommended". Very low settings need a quick, light catch. High settings are difficult to sustain with effective technique and may lead to injury. Gives a 1-minute-on, 1-minute-off test for trying settings.

### Concept2: Viewing Drag Factor
* **Citation:** Concept2. *Viewing Drag Factor*. PM5 support page, undated.
* **Get it:** [Concept2 Netherlands](https://www.concept2.nl/en/service/monitors/pm5/how-to-use/viewing-drag-factor)
* **Says:** Main Menu → More Options → Display Drag Factor, then row. The monitor shows the number after a few seconds. The damper lever adjusts it.

### Concept2: What Damper Setting and Drag Factor to Use
* **Citation:** Concept2. *What Damper Setting and Drag Factor to Use on the Concept2 RowErg*. Undated.
* **Get it:** [Concept2](https://concept2.com/training/articles/damper-setting)
* **Says:** Start at damper 3–5. A lower setting may give the best workout as technique improves. Setting the lever too high can tire muscles before the cardiovascular system gets the full benefit.

### Concept2: What is the Best Damper Setting for Me?
* **Citation:** Concept2. (2025, January 3). *What is the Best Damper Setting for Me?*
* **Get it:** [Concept2](https://concept2.com/blog/what-is-the-best-damper-setting-for-me)
* **Says:** A new RowErg reads 90 or less at damper 1 and 200 or more at damper 10. Damper numbers differ between machines, so compare by drag factor.

### Concept2: The Damper and Drag of Olympians
* **Citation:** Concept2. (2018, July 30). *The Damper and Drag of Olympians*.
* **Get it:** [Concept2](https://www.concept2.com/blog/the-damper-and-drag-of-olympians)
* **Says:** Interviews with Olympic rowers and coaches. Reported drag factors run from about 112 to 130 for the Olympians. One recommends "around 120-135 (depending on how big and strong the athlete is)" and advises testing on the setting you train with.

### Concept2: Drive Length
* **Citation:** Concept2. (2017, August 2). *Drive Length*.
* **Get it:** [Concept2](https://www.concept2.com/blog/drive-length)
* **Says:** There is no perfect drive length. The best length varies with height, leg length and flexibility. A longer drive can make a high stroke rate harder to keep. A shorter-than-normal stroke may point to poor warm-up, fatigue or a technique change.

### Concept2 UK: Indoor Rowing Training Guide, version 2
* **Citation:** O'Neill, T., & Skelton, A. *Indoor Rowing Training Guide, version 2*. Concept2 Ltd. Undated.
* **Get it:** [full guide](http://www.leicester-rowing.co.uk/media/documents/rofo/Indoor_Rowing_Training_Guide.pdf) or [shorter version](https://www.superphysique.org/images/aviron_indoor/2_Indoor_Rowing_Training_Guide_Summary.pdf) (third-party copies; drag factor sections read in both)
* **Body size:** "As a general rule, bigger heavier and stronger users would tend towards level 10 while smaller lighter users would benefit from a lower setting." This sentence appears in the full guide but not the shorter version. The guide gives no data or numbers for it and does not mention height.
* **Says:** Finding the most suitable setting is "a question of trial and error". On-water rowers use 130–140 in the full guide, and 125–140 in the shorter version.
* **Table (Amateur Rowing Association, used by Great Britain's teams):** J11/12 beginner 95–105, J12/13 105–115, J13/14 110–120, J14/15 115–125, junior women 125–135, junior men 130–140. Performance athletes: lightweight women 125, heavyweight women 130, lightweight men 135, heavyweight men 140. Both versions give the same table.
* **Note:** Kane et al. (2013) cite this guide's appendices as 2004.

### Concept2 Indoor Rower Fact Sheet 1
* **Citation:** Fletcher Sport Science. *Concept2 Indoor Rower Fact Sheet 1: Damper Lever, Drag Factor and the Speed Strap*. Undated.
* **Get it:** [PDF](https://www.leicester-rowing.co.uk/media/documents/rofo/Concept2_Setting_The_Drag_Factor.pdf) (third-party copy)
* **Says:** Adults: drag factor 115–140. On-water rowers: 125–140, because the feel is closest to a racing boat. Set the same drag factor every session. Too high a setting increases injury risk.

## Research

### Cerasola et al. (2017)
* **Citation:** Cerasola, D., Cataldo, A., Bianco, A., Zangla, D., Capranica, L., & Traina, M. (2017). Drag factor on rowing ergometer during 2000-m performance in young rowers. *Kinesiologia Slovenica*, 23(2), 15–21.
* **Get it:** [dLib Slovenia](https://www.dlib.si/details/URN:NBN:SI:DOC-PDEI3LO6) (open the PDF from this page; full text read)
* **Design:** 15 male Italian championship finalists (about 16 years, 177.4 ± 6.4 cm, 72.7 ± 9.6 kg) rowed maximal 2,000m tests at 110, 130 and 150 in randomized order, 24 hours apart.
* **Results (Table 1):**

| Drag factor | Time | Stroke rate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 110 | 435.0 ± 22.7 s | 35.1 ± 0.8 spm |
| 130 | 419.1 ± 24.1 s | 36.7 ± 0.7 spm |
| 150 | 433.7 ± 24.5 s | 32.7 ± 0.7 spm |

* **Body mass (Figure 2):** The published regression lines are time = 570.71 − 1.87 × mass at 110, 575.36 − 2.15 × mass at 130, and 588.92 − 2.13 × mass at 150. They predict 459, 446 and 461 s at 60 kg, and 403, 382 and 397 s at 90 kg, so 130 is fastest at both ends. The predictions are our arithmetic, not the authors'.
* **Says:** Heavier rowers were faster at all three settings. The authors conclude the results support 130 "independently from the anthropometric characteristics of youth athletes". They also repeat a recommendation of 100 for youth and novice rowers and 140 for experienced heavyweights, citing a 2004 Concept2 training guide and Hahn et al. (2000). The [Concept2 UK guide](#concept2-uk-indoor-rowing-training-guide-version-2) is consistent with those figures.
* **Limits:** The methods list only Pearson correlations, so no significance test of the differences between settings is reported. The study also did not test whether the best setting changes with body size. The authors call the sample small.
* **Errors in the paper:** The abstract says stroke rate was highest at 110, but its own numbers, Table 1 and the discussion show 130. The abstract gives height correlations that Table 1 lists as stroke rate vs. body mass, so we do not cite them. The 150 time's SD differs between the abstract (25.2) and Table 1 (24.5).

### Metikos et al. (2015)
* **Citation:** Metikos, B., Mikulic, P., Sarabon, N., & Markovic, G. (2015). Peak power output test on a rowing ergometer: a methodological study. *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*, 29(10), 2919–2925.
* **Get it:** [abstract](https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU201510008062). Only the abstract was read, and no open full text was found.
* **Says:** 87 participants did short maximal efforts at three settings. Rowers produced their highest power at the moderate setting, while non-rowers peaked at the highest. The abstract reports nothing on body size.

### Kane et al. (2008)
* **Citation:** Kane, D. A., Jensen, R. L., Williams, S. E., & Watts, P. B. (2008). Effects of drag factor on physiological aspects of rowing. *International Journal of Sports Medicine*, 29(5), 390–394. DOI: 10.1055/s-2007-965333
* **Get it:** [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17990205/). Only the abstract was read, and no open full text was found.
* **Says:** 14 club rowers did incremental tests at 100 and 150. Submaximal stages showed no significant differences. Maximal ventilation was higher at 100, and the ventilation difference tracked stroke rate.

### Kane et al. (2013)
* **Citation:** Kane, D. A., MacKenzie, S. J., Jensen, R. L., & Watts, P. B. (2013). Effects of stroke resistance on rowing economy in club rowers post-season. *International Journal of Sports Medicine*, 34(2), 131–137. DOI: 10.1055/s-0032-1321721
* **Get it:** [full text](http://bionics.seas.ucla.edu/education/Rowing/Training_2012_04.pdf) or [author copy](http://people.stfx.ca/smackenz/Publications/Kane%20MacKenzie%20et%20al%202012%20Effects%20of%20Stroke%20Resistance%20on%20Rowing%20Economy%20in%20Club%20Rowers%20Post-Season.pdf); [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22895868/) (full text read)
* **Design:** 12 club rowers (5 men, 7 women, 170.9 ± 2.2 cm, mean ± SEM) did randomized incremental tests at 100 and 150, one week apart. One man also did a biomechanical analysis at 100, 150 and 200.
* **Height:** "When included as a covariate, height was determined not to impact any of the variables measured or calculated."
* **Says:** The low setting gave higher stroke rate and heart rate. Economy and work per stroke were better at the high setting. Power and blood lactate did not differ.
* **Self-selected setting:** The 10 athletes who did a mid-season 2,000m chose an average of 119.0 ± 2.4 (mean ± SEM).
* **Limits:** The authors say the outcomes were not direct performance measures and that a 2,000m time trial would be more applicable. They expect the economy benefit to fade at very high resistance for individual rowers.

### Soper & Hume (2004)
* **Citation:** Soper, C., & Hume, P. A. (2004). Towards an ideal rowing technique for performance: the contributions from biomechanics. *Sports Medicine*, 34(12), 825–848. DOI: 10.2165/00007256-200434120-00003
* **Get it:** [full text](http://bionics.seas.ucla.edu/education/Rowing/Technique_2004_03.pdf) (third-party copy; searched by text)
* **Says:** A review of rowing biomechanics, covering technique and anthropometry. It does not mention drag factor, so no recommendation here relies on it.

### Kleshnev: How DF on Ergometer correlates with on-water rowing?
* **Citation:** Kleshnev, V. (2026, April 4). *How DF on Ergometer correlates with on-water rowing?* BioRow newsletter, short version.
* **Get it:** [BioRow](https://biorow.com/comparison-of-on-water-rowing-with-machines-2/) or [row2k reprint](https://www.row2k.com/features/6784/how-drag-factor-on-ergometer-correlates-with-onwater-rowing/). Only the public version was read, as the full version is for BioRow members.
* **Says:** Handle Drag Factor (HDF) depends strongly on rower weight on the water. On the erg it has almost no correlation with rower weight or power, and it tracks damper and drag factor almost perfectly. Erg settings have no one-to-one match with a boat type.

### Kleshnev: Handle Drag Factor
* **Citation:** Kleshnev, V. (2011). Q&A on drag factor. *Rowing Biomechanics Newsletter*, 11(118), January 2011.
* **Get it:** [PDF](https://old.biorow.com/RBN_en_2011_files/2011RowBiomNews01.pdf) (full text read)
* **Says:** Defines HDF as rowing power divided by the cube of average handle velocity over the drive. HDF correlates with monitor drag factor at r = 0.998. Rising drag factor lengthens drive time, raises force and slows the handle.
* **Boat table (static erg):** 127 for a single or pair, 103 for a double, 100 for a four, 86 for an eight, 84 for a quad. This assumes elite men's race speeds and 550 W.
* **Damper formula:** damper = 0.065 × drag factor − 4.32 on the ergs measured. The newsletter says to check the monitor for accuracy.
* **Limits:** It does not discuss rower height or weight.

### Dudhia: The Physics of Rowing
* **Citation:** Dudhia, A. *The Physics of Ergometers*. University of Oxford, undated.
* **Get it:** [Oxford page](http://eodg.atm.ox.ac.uk/user/dudhia/rowing/physics/ergometer.html)
* **Says:** Defines drag factor from flywheel deceleration during recovery. The monitor measures actual damping, so it accounts for vent setting and air pressure. Power follows $P = c u^3$, with Concept2 quoting $c = 2.8$. The page does not advise on choosing a drag factor.
